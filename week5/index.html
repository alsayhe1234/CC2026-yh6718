A Clock That Grows

Week 5

I was inspired by Timelines and the way it translates real-time data into visual changes. This encouraged me to think about time as something that could be experienced through movement, color, and transformation rather than through a traditional clock. My clock represents the passage of time through a flower blooming and closing, the movement of the sun and moon, and the changing colors of the sky. It begins with the user's current time using hour(), minute(), and second(), while millis() helps accelerate the passage of time so that a complete day can be experienced within a short period. During the coding process, I used an LLM as a learning tool to help me understand and build the animation step by step. I started with two simple circles representing the sun and moon, then explored how to connect their movement to time-related functions. After that, I added mouse interaction and a flower at the center, using changes in petal size, position, and rotation to represent blooming and closing. When the mouse moves closer to the flower, time gradually speeds up, causing the sun, moon, sky, and flower to change more quickly. When the mouse moves away, time returns to its normal speed. Through this interaction, I wanted to explore the question: Does time feel faster when we pay attention to it?
